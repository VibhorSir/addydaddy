Every ad account has the same obituary. "We had a winner, it scaled for six weeks, then it just died." Most people assume the market moved on. It usually didn't.

What actually happens is frequency. The same audience keeps seeing the same creative until it stops registering. Click-through drops, Meta's auction punishes the drop, cost per acquisition climbs, and the account spends more to get less. The ad didn't die. It got tired.

The tell is predictable. A creative that held a 3x ROAS at frequency 1.5 starts slipping at frequency 3 and falls off a cliff past 4. By the time you notice, you've been bleeding for two weeks.

## Why this kills accounts

The standard response to a fading winner is to pour more budget into it, which makes the frequency problem worse, faster. I've watched brands take a healthy account and torch it in a month by doubling spend on a creative that was already fatiguing.

The second mistake is waiting for the winner to die before you start making new work. Creative production is slow. If you start refreshing the day the numbers drop, you've already given back two or three weeks of margin.

## A cadence that works

We run creative testing weekly, not monthly. Every Monday we ship a batch of new hooks against the current winner. Most of them fail. That's the point. We're not trying to find the next hero ad, we're trying to keep a pipeline full so there's always something fresh to rotate in when frequency climbs.

We watch frequency and cost per acquisition together. When frequency crosses the point where CPA starts rising on a specific ad set, we rotate the creative before the account-wide numbers move. Cheap to do, and it keeps the auction healthy.

We also refresh the winner's hook and thumbnail long before the body copy. Most of the fatigue lives in the first three seconds. You can often keep a winning ad alive for weeks by changing the hook and leaving the proof alone.

## The uncomfortable part

Creative is now the main lever in paid media. Audiences are broad, targeting is locked down, and the auction mostly runs itself. The only real edge left is how fast you can make good creative and how disciplined you are about rotating it.

If you're still running the same three ads you launched two months ago, the account isn't fine. It's just not dead yet.
