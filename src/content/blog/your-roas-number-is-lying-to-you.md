I audited a D2C brand earlier this year that told me they were running 4.2x blended ROAS. The founder was proud of it, and on paper he had every right to be. Then I split the account by SKU.

One hero product was doing 9x. Everything else sat around 1.3x, and that 1.3x was eating 60% of the budget.

Blended ROAS is an average, and averages hide more than they reveal. When you scale, you don't scale the average. You scale whatever you buy next, which is usually the marginal product, not the hero SKU that's already maxed out.

I've watched accounts where blended ROAS went up while the business quietly lost money. The winning product got more budget, looked brilliant, and masked the fact that the rest of the catalog was being subsidized. The dashboard said 4x. The bank account said otherwise.

## What to track instead

The first number is contribution margin per order. ROAS doesn't know your product costs. A 3x on a high-margin skincare item and a 5x on a low-margin snack are not the same business. Take revenue, subtract COGS, shipping, and payment gateway fees, then divide by spend. That's the number that pays salaries.

The second is payback period: how many days until the revenue from a new customer covers what you paid to get them. If your supplier terms are net 45 and your payback is 60 days, you are growing yourself into a cash crunch. This is the number most founders ignore until they can't ignore it anymore.

The third is marginal ROAS, not blended. Before you push more budget into a campaign, ask what the next rupee actually buys. Look at the ROAS of the last 20% of spend you added, not the account total. That's the real curve you're riding.

## The fix is boring

Split reporting by product line and by new versus returning customers. Kill the campaigns that only look good because they're blended with a winner. Pick a target that accounts for margin and cash, not a vanity number you saw in someone's LinkedIn post.

The brands that scale without blowing up are the ones tracking contribution margin per order every single day. Everyone else is just keeping score with the wrong number.
