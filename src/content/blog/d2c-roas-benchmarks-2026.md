Every founder asks the same question in the first call: what's a good ROAS for us? Fair question. The answer they usually want is a number they can screenshot and send to their investors. The honest answer is messier.

Here are the real numbers for 2026, then why chasing them will send you the wrong way.

## The headline numbers

The average ecommerce ROAS in 2026 is around 2.87x, according to [Hawky's benchmark data](https://hawky.ai/blog/average-roas-ecommerce-benchmarks). The median is only 2.04x. That gap matters. The average gets pulled up by a handful of high performers, so if you're sitting at the "average" 2.87x, you're actually doing better than most stores. Half of ecommerce businesses run below 2x.

[Littledata's Shopify benchmark](https://www.littledata.io/ecommerce-conversion-rate) tells the same story on the conversion side. The average Shopify store converts at 1.4%. The top 20% convert at 3.2% or higher, and the top 10% at 4.7% or higher. Most brands are not at 3%. They're at 1.5% and pretending the benchmark doesn't exist.

## Platform matters more than the headline

ROAS is not one number. It splits hard by channel.

Google Ads runs at a median around 3.5x, higher than Meta, because search captures people already looking to buy. [Focus Digital's 2026 report](https://focus-digital.co/average-roas-google-ads/) puts Google's median at 3.52x versus Meta's 2.21x.

Meta splits further. Prospecting runs around 2.2x, retargeting around 3.6x. If your blended Meta number is 3x, you're not doing 3x on cold traffic. You're doing 2x on cold and 4x on warm, and the warm audience is small. Meta's Advantage+ Shopping campaigns report roughly 20% better ROAS than manual structures, which is why we default to them for catalogue-heavy D2C accounts.

"What's a good ROAS" has no single answer until you say which platform and which audience.

## India's numbers are lower, and that's fine

Most benchmark reports are global and skew US. Indian D2C runs lower on paper. Industry chatter and a few published analyses put Indian D2C conversion around 1%, with growth funded by paid spend rather than organic maturity.

Cash on delivery drags it down further. A 30% return-to-origin rate on COD orders quietly destroys the ROAS of your paid campaigns, because the revenue your dashboard reports never actually lands. If you compare your India numbers to a US benchmark and panic, you're benchmarking the wrong thing.

## The number ROAS never tells you

ROAS is revenue divided by spend. It says nothing about margin.

A 4x ROAS on a product with 20% margins loses money. A 2x ROAS on a 65% margin product prints. The only number that pays salaries is contribution margin per order: revenue minus product cost, shipping, and payment gateway fees, divided by spend.

Then there's payback period. How many days until a new customer's revenue covers what you spent to get them. If your supplier terms are net 45 and your payback is 60 days, you're growing yourself into a cash crunch, no matter what the ROAS says.

## How to actually use benchmarks

Use them as a sanity check, not a target. If your Meta prospecting is at 1.5x and the median is 2.2x, something is off: creative, offer, or tracking. Go fix that. If you're at 2.5x and your contribution margin is healthy, you're fine, even if a competitor brags about 5x on LinkedIn.

Benchmark your own account first. Split blended ROAS by product line and by new versus returning customers. Watch the marginal ROAS on the last 20% of spend you add, not the account average. Track contribution margin and payback, not just ROAS.

The brands that scale are the ones who stopped asking for a magic number and started tracking the math that actually shows up in the bank account.
