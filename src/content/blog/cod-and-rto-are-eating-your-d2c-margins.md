If you sell online in India, you already know the tradeoff. Cash on delivery lifts conversion, sometimes by 20 to 40 percent. But a chunk of those COD orders never get delivered. The courier shows up, the customer changed their mind or wasn't home, and the parcel comes back to you. That's return to origin, and you paid for the shipping both ways.

I've seen RTO rates above 30 percent on COD-heavy catalogs. Run that math. On a product with a 30 percent margin, a 30 percent RTO rate wipes out almost all the profit on your COD orders. You're not growing. You're running a logistics charity.

## Where the money actually leaks

It's not one big hole. It's several small ones stacked together.

Bad addresses are the quietest one. A pincode typo or a missing landmark turns a good order into a return that was never going to deliver. Cleaning address data at checkout, and validating pincodes before the order confirms, removes a share of returns before they happen.

Then there's buyer hesitation. A lot of COD orders are placed on impulse. By the time the courier arrives, the customer has cooled off. A quick WhatsApp or SMS confirmation after checkout, done politely and fast, catches most of the change-of-mind cases before dispatch.

Then the prepaid nudge. Even a small discount or a small freebie for paying upfront moves a meaningful slice of orders from COD to prepaid, where RTO basically disappears. You don't need to convert everyone. Shifting 10 to 15 percent of orders is usually enough to change the whole unit economics.

## The tools do the heavy lifting

This is a solved problem at the tooling layer. GoKwik and similar checkout products handle address validation, COD confirmation flows, and prepaid nudges out of the box. Shiprocket handles the fulfilment side, including the carrier logic that routes a parcel to whichever partner actually delivers to that pincode. Netcore and the lifecycle tools handle the post-purchase messaging that keeps buyers from changing their mind.

None of this is magic. It's just work that most brands don't do because they're staring at top-line revenue and ROAS, not at what survives to the bank account.

## Start with one number

Pick one thing to fix first. If RTO is above 20 percent, the confirmation flow is almost always the fastest win. It's cheap to set up and the results show up within a week or two.

Measure RTO by product and by pincode, not just as an account-level average, because it hides the same way blended ROAS does. The brands that fix this stop burning money on returns. The ones that don't keep wondering why their profitable ads never turn into profit.
